Preparing to Coach

Required Reading (in Skills & Strategies for Coaching Soccer, Second Edition)

  • Chapter 1: Ethics and Your Coaching Philosophy (pp. 4-7)
  • Chapter 2: Personal Coaching Strategies (pp. 10-15)
  • Chapter 3: Group Coaching Strategies (pp. 18-24)

Learning Objectives

  • Discuss the importance of putting winning in the proper perspective and developing your personal coaching philosophy.
  • Identify the basic principles of coaching.
  • Ddentify the basic principles of coaching.

Optional Reading

We recommend that you review part I, Foundational Skills for Coaches (pp. 1-59), in Successful Coaching, Fifth Edition.

Learning Objectives

  • Develop your personal coaching philosophy.
  • Determine your coaching objectives.
  • Select a coaching style that fits your personality and complements your coaching philosophy.
  • Explain the elements of coaching diverse athletes.

Practicum 1

Develop and record the following items before you begin your next sport season:

  • Your personal coaching philosophy
  • A mission statement for your sport program
  • Athlete code of conduct

You should evaluate your philosophy, mission statement, and athlete code of conduct each year. If necessary, make adjustments to meet the specific needs and circumstances of your sport program.

Practicum 2

Develop an annual plan that includes the following:

  • Calendar on which you list out-of-season activities, preseason activities, in-season plans, and postseason plans
  • Budget
  • Camps and clinics
  • Evaluations
  • Fundraising
  • Parent-player meetings

This plan should become a checklist that guides your program throughout the entire year.

Practicum 3

Develop an agenda for your preseason parent-player meeting. Create the following handouts:

  • Team rules
  • Player-parent contract
  • In-season hydration guide

Include any additional handouts that you would distribute during your first meeting with your players and their parents.

Skills and Techniques

Required Reading (in Skills & Strategies for Coaching Soccer, Second Edition)

  • Chapter 4: Collecting and Controlling the Ball (pp.28-54)
  • Chapter 5: Passing (pp.56-94)
  • Chapter 6: Dribbling (pp.96-121)
  • Chapter 7: Striking the Ball (pp.124-139)
  • Chapter 8: Shooting (pp.142-156)
  • Chapter 9: Heading (pp.158-171)
  • Chapter 10: Goalkeeping (pp.174-198)
  • Chapter 11: Tackling and Defensive Skills (pp.200-230)

Learning Objectives

Chapter 4
  • Recognize and teach the skills necessary for effective ball control:
  • Explain how club face direction and club path direction affect the direction of the ball.
    • Ball juggling
    • Collecting and controlling techniques
    • Receiving the ball under pressure
    • Controlling and passing on the move
Chapter 5
  • Recognize and teach the basic techniques of short passing:
    • Push pass
    • Outside of the foot pass
    • Instep pass
  • Recognize and teach the basic techniques of long passing.
  • Introduce decision-making situations for both the passer and the receiver.
Chapter 6
  • Teach the physical skills of dribbling:
    • Keeping the ball within playing distance
    • Remaining balanced
    • Practiced moves
    • Seeing the ball
  • Teach the mental aspects of dribbling:
    • Confidence
    • Opponent positioning
    • React to opponent’s movement
Chapter 7
  • Recognize and teach the various ways of striking the ball:
    • Instep kick
    • Volley and half-volley
    • Chip
    • Swerve
    • Banana
Chapter 8
  • Recognize and teach the skill of shooting:
    • Where and how to aim
    • When to shoot
    • How to encourage players to stay calm and composed
Chapter 9
  • Recognize and teach the skill of heading:
    • Development of sensitive progression drills for beginners
    • Attacking and defensive heading
    • Diving technique
    • Standing technique
Chapter 10
  • Recognize and teach the skill of goalkeeping:
    • Basic stance
    • Catching the ball
    • Ground shots
    • Diving saves
    • Crosses
  • Recognize and teach the skill of supporting the defense:
    • Judging angles
    • Distributing the ball by kicking
    • Distributing the ball by hand
Chapter 11
  • Recognize and teach first defender skills:
    • Closing down on an attacker
    • Positioning and interceptions
    • Defending an attacker when turning
    • Redirecting an attacker
    • Recovering when beaten
  • Recognize and teach second defender skills:
    • Covering and supporting
    • Setting the offside trap
  • Examine the techniques of the three basic tackles:
    • Block tackle
    • Side block tackle
    • Sliding tackle

Optional Reading

We recommend that you review the following chapters in part III, Being a Great Teacher, in Successful Coaching, Fifth Edition:

  • Chapter 9, The Games Approach (pp. 135-147)
  • Chapter 10, Teaching Technical Skills (pp. 149-165)

Learning Objectives

  • Determine how you will incorporate the games approach into your practice plans.
  • Describe how to effectively teach technical skills.

Practicum 4

Select a skill that you'll teach your athletes early in the season. Then write down

  • a description of the skill,
  • how you'll introduce the skill,
  • how you'll demonstrate the skill,
  • how you'll teach the skill, and
  • the drills you'll use to practice the skill.

Once your season begins and you have included this skill in a practice plan, evaluate the effectiveness of your plan and make the necessary adjustments that will enhance the learning experience of your athletes.

Tactics and Teamwork

Required Reading (in Skills & Strategies for Coaching Soccer, Second Edition)

  • Chapter 12: Principles of Play (pp.234-241)
  • Chapter 13: Team Formations and Systems of Play (pp.244-263)
  • Chapter 14: Coaching Teamwork (pp.266-273)
  • Chapter 15: Tactics in Defense, Midfield, and Attack (pp.276-286)
  • Chapter 16: Set Pieces (pp.288-320)

Learning Objectives

Chapter 12
  • Be able to introduce the concepts related to team play.
  • Recognize the nine principles of team play.
  • List and discuss the three principles common to both attack and defense:
    • Possession
    • Support
    • Communication
  • Explain the six principles of attack and defense:
    • Delay in defense
    • Penetration in attack
    • Concentration in defense
    • Width in attack
    • Mobility in attack
    • Balance in defense
Chapter 13
  • Analyze the four most common modern systems of play:
    • 4-3-3
    • 4-2-4
    • 3-3-4
    • 4-4-2
  • Determine how to select a system that best accommodates the strengths and weaknesses of individual players.
  • Design or modify a system to suit the needs of your team.
Chapter 14
  • Apply the techniques and strategies of planning a practice:
    • What do I coach?
    • Where do I coach?
    • How do I coach?
Chapter 15
  • Introduce advanced tactics in defense, midfield, and attack.
Chapter 16
  • Introduce and examine a series of moves and strategies for three kinds of set plays:
    • Throw-ins
    • Corner kicks
    • Free kicks

Optional Reading

We recommend that you review the following chapters in part III, Being a Great Teacher, in Successful Coaching, Fifth Edition:

  • Chapter 11, Teaching Tactical Skills (pp. 167-186)
  • Chapter 12, Planning for Teaching (pp. 187-204)

Learning Objectives

  • Describe how to effectively teach tactical skills.
  • List the benefits of planning for teaching.

Practicum 5

Develop two complete practice plans that include the following:

  • Preseason practice plan
    • Warm-up
    • Introduction of a new technical skill
    • Games approach
    • Conditioning
    • Cool-down
  • In-season practice plan
    • Warm-up
    • Introduction of a new technical skill
    • Games approach
    • Controlled scrimmage
    • Cool-down

Preseason practice plans should be based on the season plan you developed during the off-season. An in-season practice plan should be based on the adjustments you need to make from your last game and the scouting report for your next opponent.

Team Management

Required Reading (in Skills & Strategies for Coaching Soccer, Second Edition)

  • Chapter 17: Physical and Mental Preparation for Games (pp.326-331)
  • Chapter 18: Working with Problem Players and Parents (pp.334-342)
  • Chapter 19: Fitness and Conditioning for Soccer (pp.344-364)

Learning Objectives

Chapter 17
  • Explain how to prepare your athletes physically for a game:
    • Warm-up
    • Cool-down
    • Ball skills
  • Explain how to prepare your athletes physically for a game:
    • Pregame meeting
    • Communication during the game
    • Postgame meeting
Chapter 18
  • Identify the types of players that can be difficult to coach.
  • Explore ways of working with difficult players and their parents.
Chapter 19
  • Identify the fitness elements that collectively produce well-conditioned athletes.
  • Understand the importance of diet, nutrition, and hydration to your athletes.

Optional Reading

We recommend that you review part II, Understanding and Managing Athletes’ Behavior (pp. 59-131), in Successful Coaching, Fifth Edition.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the value of character development for your athletes and select character development skills to teach.
  • Identify ways of communicating effectively with your athletes.
  • Outline strategies for motivating your athletes.
  • Describe methods of managing your athletes’ behavior.

Optional Reading

We recommend that you review part IV, Physical Training and Nutrition (pp. 205-315), in Successful Coaching, Fifth Edition.

Learning Objectives

  • Outline the basics of training the body for physical activity.
  • Explain the concept of energy fitness.
  • Describe ways of increasing muscular fitness and why it's important for your athletes.
  • Explain how the body fuels itself.
  • Understand your role in preventing substance abuse in your athletes.

Practicum 6

Describe the building blocks of a successful sport program. Consider the following:

  • Surround yourself with good people
  • Develop a feeder system
  • Earn the respect of your athletes
  • Pinpoint leaders
  • Facility updates and accessibility
  • Program promotion
  • Team rules
  • Developing champions on and off the field

Expand on these building blocks of a successful program, or create your own.

Practicum 7

Research the rules and regulations that govern athletes in your sport. Include state as well as local policies. The following are some areas to look into as you begin. As you research the regulations in these areas, you'll probably want to expand and add additional items:

  • Academic eligibility
  • Health and immunization records
  • Off-season participation
  • Transfer rules

Practicum 8

Develop a plan to include positive parent involvement in your program:

  • Awards
  • Concessions
  • Fundraising
  • Event management
  • Team meals
  • Transportation

Expand on these ideas or create your own plans to involve your parents in a positive way.

Practicum 9

Develop a facilities checklist to ensure you're providing a safe practice and meet environment for your athletes::

  • Gymnasium
  • Locker rooms
  • Field or outside playing area
  • Pool
  • Track

Create a checklist for each of the facilities or areas that pertain to your sport.

Practicum 10

Write up season summaries and program evaluations.

For record-keeping purposes, it's very important to have a season summary and evaluation on file in the athletic office. Develop an outline of items to include in your summary:

  • Award winners
  • Win-loss record
  • Win-loss record for lower-level teams
  • Program strengths
  • Program weaknesses

Expand on these ideas and add your own summary and evaluation items.
